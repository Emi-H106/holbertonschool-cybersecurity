## Communication Sequence and Governance

Immediate P1 findings must be communicated to the CISO and their responsible operational or technical leaders without waiting for completion of the final report.

The formal report should first establish the confirmed technical findings and their evidence boundaries, then explain the conditional attack chains that depend on them. This prevents hypothetical attack progression from being presented as demonstrated exploitation.

Executive communications should prioritize patient-data confidentiality, diagnostic workflow integrity, business continuity, financial exposure, and decisions requiring management approval.

Technical communications should provide sufficient evidence for remediation implementation and verification, including attack prerequisites, compensating controls, affected components, and acceptance criteria.

The DPO and Compliance and HDS Manager should receive relevant findings early enough to assess GDPR obligations, retention requirements, audit evidence, and the upcoming HDS renewal. A vulnerability does not automatically establish a reportable personal-data breach or certification failure.

The CFO should receive the financial-impact scenarios together with their assumptions and uncertainty, particularly for the attack chains. The conditional ranges must not be presented as expected losses or added together because the scenarios overlap.

The CISO should consolidate technical remediation dependencies, unresolved validation questions, and residual risks for the Technical Director, CEO, CFO, and audit committee.

The final governance discussion should identify approved remediation priorities, accountable implementation owners, budget decisions, verification evidence, and any residual risks requiring explicit management acceptance within MediPath's eight-week audit-committee decision window.
