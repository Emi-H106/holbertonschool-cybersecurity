
# Source Material Critical Review

| Finding | First impression | Zone of doubt or confidence | To investigate in Task 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F-01 | Sarah demonstrated that a laboratory operator could retrieve another tenant's diagnostic result by changing the result identifier. | The cross-tenant access is convincingly evidenced, while the environmental significance of patient-data exposure needs verification. | Results API tenant-authorization design, exposed patient fields, identifier discoverability, the 400-laboratory footprint, anomaly detection, and health-data confidentiality requirements. |
| F-02 | Sarah showed that stored laboratory notes execute active content when opened by a support analyst. | The execution evidence is persuasive, but the support portal's effective privileges and browser defenses warrant closer review. | Support analysts' cross-tenant access, managed-workstation and MFA controls, Content Security Policy enforcement, and access to sensitive support-session data. |
| F-03 | Sarah demonstrated that a 180-day hospital connector token with `integration:all` scope could access another connector's functions. | Cross-connector authorization failure is supported by the test, but token reachability and revocation controls remain uncertain. | Gateway network restrictions, connector-specific audience and tenant enforcement, rotation and revocation practices, and any shared authorization mechanisms with F-09. |
| F-04 | Sarah confirmed that the legacy connector worker disables upstream certificate peer verification through `verify_peer=false`. | The configuration weakness is established, but its live exploitability and the High rating remain uncertain because a managed gateway reportedly validates upstream certificates. | Actual worker-to-gateway traffic path, mutual TLS and upstream certificate validation, destination restrictions, gateway bypass routes, monitoring, and failure scenarios. |
| F-05 | Sarah demonstrated that the partner webhook accepted an unsigned request changing a synthetic sample from `received` to `validated`. | The integrity weakness is demonstrated from an approved source range, but the practical impact depends on network controls and downstream workflow trust. | Source-IP allowlist enforcement, partner identity binding, sample-state transition controls, and whether validation status triggers result release or hospital exports. |
| F-06 | Sarah found that a diagnostic export remained downloadable after 29 days despite a documented 24-hour expiry. | The retention mismatch is evidenced, while the severity depends on the approved retention rules and actual access exposure. | DPO-approved retention schedule, signed-link generation and access permissions, export volume and sensitivity, access logging, deletion controls, and RGPD/HDS accountability. |
| F-07 | Sarah used a pre-approved Support Manager test role to generate a multi-laboratory patient export through the bulk-export workflow. | The export capability is proven, but whether it is a vulnerability is unresolved because the client describes an authorized, multi-approval emergency workflow. | Mandatory DPO ticket, WebAuthn step-up, dual approval on every production path, case-specific encryption, seven-day expiry, immutable audit logs, and potential bypasses. |
| F-08 | Sarah observed distinct password-recovery responses for registered and unregistered laboratory accounts, enabling account enumeration. | The information disclosure is well supported, but its practical significance requires assessment against identity protections. | Laboratory-user identity sensitivity, SSO coverage, MFA adoption, account-aware throttling, monitoring, and potential targeted identity or support attacks. |
| F-09 | Sarah demonstrated that a downgraded administrator retained connector-profile write access in an existing session for approximately 27 minutes. | The stale-authorization window is convincingly demonstrated, while privileged-action safeguards and its relationship to F-03 remain open questions. | Distributed authorization-cache behavior, session and token revocation, frequency of role changes, privileged-action approvals and logging, and possible shared identity-governance root causes with F-03. |
| F-10 | Sarah demonstrated that a direct application-network request could supply `X-Lab-ID` and retrieve another laboratory's aggregate analytics. | The gateway-trust bypass is evidenced, but the scope of reachable callers and any cross-finding attack chain require verification. | Application-network reachability, service-to-service authentication, trusted tenant-claim enforcement, possible access via F-03 tokens, and whether analytics identifiers facilitate F-01 result discovery. |
