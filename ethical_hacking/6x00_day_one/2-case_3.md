
# Case 3: The Overreach
## Post-Incident Analysis

### 1. What Happened

InnovateTech hired Apex Security Partners for a red team assessment without clearly defining the expected activities. Apex interpreted the engagement as authorization for a full adversary simulation, including physical intrusion. During testing, a consultant impersonated a delivery courier, entered the headquarters through a secured door, and accessed a server room. Building security discovered the intrusion, and the police were called. The client had never explicitly approved physical testing, resulting in termination of the engagement and serious reputational damage to Apex.

### 2. Rules, Laws, or Professional Standards Violated

- **Unclear Authorization:** The contract did not explicitly authorize physical intrusion testing.
- **Scope and Rules of Engagement:** The engagement lacked clearly defined physical locations, permitted techniques, and testing boundaries.
- **Potential Unauthorized Entry:** Entering restricted areas without valid permission could violate applicable trespass or unauthorized access laws.
- **PTES Pre-Engagement Standard:** Apex failed to clarify the client's objectives and establish appropriate testing rules.
- **Professional Ethics:** Apex assumed permission instead of confirming the client's expectations and obtaining explicit approval.

### 3. Correct Professional Behavior

Apex should have clarified the client's expectations during the initial scoping discussions.

The team should have explained the differences between penetration testing and red teaming, including the possible use of social engineering and physical intrusion.

Physical testing should have been excluded unless the client explicitly requested and authorized it.

If physical testing was approved, Apex should have obtained a signed authorization letter specifying permitted locations, techniques, testing windows, and emergency contacts.

The team should also have established procedures for interactions with security personnel and law enforcement.

### 4. Preventive Pre-Engagement Clauses

**Engagement Objectives Clause:**
"The engagement objectives, expected outcomes, and authorized testing activities must be clearly defined and agreed upon before testing begins."

**Physical Security Authorization Clause:**
"Physical intrusion testing is prohibited unless explicitly authorized in writing through a separate physical security testing agreement."

**Permitted Techniques Clause:**
"Authorized techniques, including phishing, pretexting, tailgating, and physical access attempts, must be individually approved before execution."

**Physical Scope Clause:**
"All authorized buildings, rooms, access methods, and testing windows must be explicitly documented."

**Emergency and Law Enforcement Clause:**
"The engagement must define emergency contacts, identification procedures, and escalation steps for interactions with security personnel or law enforcement."

**Authorization Letter Clause:**
"Consultants performing approved physical testing must carry a signed authorization letter identifying the engagement and relevant verification contacts. This letter does not provide immunity from law enforcement action."

**Conclusion:** The term "red team" does not automatically authorize every attack technique. All activities must be explicitly agreed upon and documented.
