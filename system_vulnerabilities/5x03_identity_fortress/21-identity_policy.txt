NOVATECH SOLUTIONS
IDENTITY SECURITY POLICY

Document Owner: Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
Version: 1.0
Status: Draft for Approval
Scope: All employees, contractors, administrators, service accounts, and systems
Review Cycle: Annually and after significant security incidents or changes

1. PURPOSE AND RESPONSIBILITIES
This policy establishes minimum requirements for identity management,
authentication, privileged access, and account recovery. All users must
comply. IT administers access controls; managers approve business access;
the Security team monitors compliance and investigates exceptions.

2. PASSWORD POLICY (COMPLEXITY, HISTORY, AGING)
- Passwords must be at least 14 characters long; longer passphrases are encouraged.
- Use a mix of character types where required by the system; prioritize
  length, uniqueness, and resistance to commonly used or breached passwords.
- Passwords must not contain easily guessed personal or company information.
- Reuse of the previous 10 passwords is prohibited where technically supported.
- Default and temporary passwords must be changed at first use.
- Routine expiration is not required for ordinary user passwords unless
  mandated by a specific compliance obligation. Reset passwords immediately
  if compromise is suspected, after confirmed disclosure, or when required
  by an approved risk assessment.
- Passwords must not be shared or stored in plaintext. Approved password
  managers are permitted and encouraged.

3. ACCOUNT LOCKOUT POLICY
- After 5 consecutive failed authentication attempts, the account must be
  temporarily locked for at least 15 minutes, or until an authorized reset.
- Failed-login counters should reset after a defined observation period,
  normally 15 minutes without further failures.
- Authentication failures and lockouts must be logged and monitored.
- Security must investigate repeated failures, distributed guessing, and
  suspicious lockouts. Unlock requests require identity verification.
- Controls must account for denial-of-service risks from deliberate lockouts.

4. SSH ACCESS POLICY
- SSH access is permitted only for authorized users and approved systems.
- SSH public-key authentication is required; password-based SSH login is
  disabled on managed servers.
- ED25519 keys are preferred. Private keys must be protected, never shared,
  and stored with restrictive file permissions.
- SSH authorized_keys must be reviewed periodically and stale keys removed.
- Direct root SSH login is prohibited; administrators use named accounts
  and approved sudo elevation with appropriate logging.
- SSH services must use supported versions, approved network restrictions,
  and secure configurations. Configuration changes require validation
  (sshd -t), a backup, and a tested recovery path before reload.

5. MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION (MFA) REQUIREMENTS
- MFA is mandatory for privileged accounts, remote administrative access,
  and other high-risk systems designated by Security.
- SSH administrative access requires both a valid public key and a separate
  TOTP factor, enforced through PAM and AuthenticationMethods where deployed.
- Each user must enroll their own authenticator; TOTP secrets and recovery
  codes must be stored securely and never shared.
- MFA enrollment and reset require identity verification. Any exception
  must be approved, documented, time-limited, and monitored.
- Loss of an authenticator must be reported promptly to IT/Security.

6. ACCOUNT LIFECYCLE
Creation:
- Accounts require a documented request, manager approval, a unique user
  identity, and least-privilege access appropriate to the role.
- Privileged access requires additional approval and MFA enrollment.
Modification:
- Access changes due to transfers or role changes require documented
  authorization. Remove permissions no longer needed promptly.
- Access rights must be reviewed periodically, at least quarterly for
  privileged accounts and annually for standard accounts.
Termination:
- Disable interactive access and revoke active sessions, SSH keys, tokens,
  and MFA credentials promptly upon termination or loss of authorization.
- Transfer ownership of business data according to retention requirements.
- Delete accounts only after applicable legal, operational, and audit
  retention obligations have been satisfied.
Service accounts:
- Must have an owner, narrowly scoped permissions, no unnecessary
  interactive login, and documented credential rotation procedures.

7. EMERGENCY ACCESS PROCEDURES (SUMMARY)
- A user who loses a TOTP device must report it immediately and complete
  identity verification through an approved independent channel.
- Use a previously issued, securely stored, single-use emergency scratch
  code where available. Re-enroll MFA and replace recovery codes afterward.
- If recovery codes are unavailable, authorized administrators may use
  controlled console or out-of-band access to restore the user's MFA.
- Temporary MFA bypass is a last resort: require incident/ticket tracking,
  Security approval, a narrow scope, an expiration time, and monitoring.
  Restore MFA immediately after recovery and validate access controls.
- Root recovery must use authorized console or recovery mechanisms, never
  shared root credentials or unrecorded bypasses.
- Record the requester, approver, affected accounts/systems, justification,
  timestamps, actions taken, and restoration confirmation. Protect logs
  from alteration; never record secrets or one-time codes.
- Refer to 20-emergency_procedures.txt for the detailed procedure.

8. COMPLIANCE, EXCEPTIONS, AND REVIEW
- Violations must be reported to Security and addressed according to
  organizational disciplinary and incident-response procedures.
- Exceptions require written CISO or delegated Security approval, a risk
  assessment, compensating controls, and a defined expiration/review date.
- IT and Security will review this policy at least annually and update it
  when threats, systems, or legal obligations change.
